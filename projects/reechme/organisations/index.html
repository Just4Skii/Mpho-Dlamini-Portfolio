For organisations

Support people. Don’t watch them.

ReechMe is a bounded pilot concept for organisations that already provide support. It is designed to make a trusted-contact action clearer — not to create a monitoring product, a case file, or a substitute for emergency services.
No surveillanceConsent-firstNo personal data required

Current status

  • Browser prototype, Version 2.1
  • No live service, backend, or integrations
  • Not certified, audited, or clinically validated
  • No pilot partners have been confirmed

Where it could fit

Adjacent to support, not instead of it.

These are discussion contexts, not claimed deployments. Any real pilot needs the organisation’s existing safeguarding, clinical, and legal governance to lead.

Campuses

A student-facing support pathway that does not require a public disclosure to start. Integrate only where student consent and campus safeguarding are already clear.

NGOs and support services

A demonstration tool for co-designing what a trusted-contact alert should say, and what it must never imply. Not a case-management system.

Clinics and care settings

A discussion aid for discharge planning and safety planning, with the boundary that the prototype provides no clinical record and no clinical claim.

Workplaces

An employee-support concept for organisations that already have trained responders. It must never become performance monitoring or location surveillance.

The boundary

A safety feature is not a monitoring feature.

The product model keeps the device owner in control. It does not expose a dashboard of people, locations, or risk scores to an organisation.

No admin view. The prototype has no organisation dashboard, account system, or reporting export.

The user chooses the circle. Trusted contacts are configured by the person using the service, not assigned by an employer or institution.

No evidence claims. Nothing in the demo is designed to record, verify, or preserve evidence for a disciplinary or legal process.

A useful pilot, and a harmful one

Plausible

  • Co-designing a consent-first trusted-contact flow with the people who would use it.
  • Testing whether clearer delivery language reduces false confidence after an alert.
  • Exploring a keypad-phone channel for people who do not use a smartphone.

Off the table

  • Monitoring employees, students, patients, or partners without their knowledge.
  • Replacing an emergency line, a safeguarding team, or a clinical risk assessment.
  • Collecting evidence, recording incidents, or making disciplinary decisions.

Pilot readiness

What we would need before a real deployment.

This checklist is deliberately demanding. The prototype is not ready for use with people at risk, and a pilot should not start until these questions have owners and written answers.

No confirmed partners

ReechMe has not partnered with, been endorsed by, or been procured by any organisation. Nothing on this page should be read as a case study or a deployment claim.

  • 01

    A named organisation sponsor and a clear problem statement.

  • 02

    A safeguarding lead and an escalation path for participants who disclose risk.

  • 03

    A consent process that explains exactly what the demo does and does not do.

  • 04

    A data-minimisation review — the prototype needs no personal data.

  • 05

    A plan for co-design participants from the groups most likely to be excluded.

  • 06

    Independent accessibility testing before any supported use.

  • 07

    Legal review of jurisdiction, employment, health, and data obligations.

  • 08

    A published evaluation question, with success and stop criteria agreed in advance.

Start a conversation

Bring a real safeguarding question, not a feature request.

The most useful next step is a structured critique: which groups are excluded, what an alert should never claim, and how a trusted-contact flow interacts with existing support. The contact form is a non-sending demonstration.

Clear about what this is

A product and interaction prototype built from a Version 2.1 blueprint. It is not a company, a service, a legal entity, or an emergency provider.

The contact form does not send, store, or log anything. Do not enter confidential or personal information.
